# The village vocabulary. What Amrai Kheda must contain. Her brief, 6 Nov.

Her words: "houses look extremely bad. I liked earlier houses. At least they had variety
in them, they had different textures in them, and there were things happening outside
the house, a cycle parked, maybe a pot, flower pot kept, and the gobar... There were people
moving around in the village... you have to put ice cream shop, you have to put more
shops, you have to show more vendors with not closed shops but open shops. You need to
put a barber who sits under the tree."

Every item below is UP-specific and cheap. Everything is instanced and varied in turn,
tint and scale. Two identical neighbours is a failure. The game's existing look stays;
the mood board was for LIFE and DENSITY only.

## 1. OUTSIDE EVERY HOUSE. This is what fixes the houses. Each house gets 3 to 6 of these.
- charpai in use, or leaning against the wall on its side
- cycle leaning against the wall
- matka on a stand with a lota; tulsi on its plinth in the angan
- gobar cakes slapped on the wall; a bitauda stack in a corner (both already built)
- red chillies or grain drying on a charpai or on the roof
- washing on the parapet and on a line (laundry_line exists)
- flower pots in old paint tins; a gourd vine over the wall
- painted door: swastik, shubh-labh, mango-leaf toran
- hand-painted wall adverts: a cement brand, a tonic, a faded election symbol
- blue roof tank, dish antenna, tangled wires to the pole, electric meter box
- a house number painted on the wall; a nameplate

## 2. HOUSE VARIETY. Put back what the earlier houses had.
bg_house_a..d, abadi_row_a..c, kachcha_homestead and house_compound are all still in the
repo. They had the variety and the textures she liked. Compare them with house_kit_a..h,
write down what the kit lacks, and put it back: real double storey beside single, outside
stairs to the roof, chajjas, parapets with openings, painted skirting bands, pukka beside
kachcha. Reuse the old house TEXTURES on the kit, not flat colours.

## 3. ANIMALS. Almost free, and the strongest memory triggers.
- buffalo tied at a khoonta with a trough, dung around it
- dog asleep under a cart or in the shade (she will see the afternoon in that one dog)
- goats on a wall, hens in a yard, a tied calf
- PEACOCK on a roof or a wall. The most UP thing possible. Build it.
- MONKEYS on a roof or a water tank
- buffaloes wallowing in the pond; a dhobi beating clothes at its edge

## 4. SHOPS AND VENDORS. All OPEN, goods spilling outward.
- ICE CREAM CART with a bell (she asked for it by name)
- BARBER UNDER A TREE: chair, mirror nailed to the trunk, towel, a customer (named twice)
- paan shop: mirror, film posters, hanging gutkha strips, a bulb
- kirana with shampoo sachets and chip packets hanging in strips
- halwai: big kadhai outside, jalebi tray on a stand IN THE STREET in front of his shop
- chai stall: kulhads, a bench, men sitting on it
- tailor with his machine in the doorway, a shirt on a hanger
- cycle repair with tyres hanging, a tube in a tub of water
- mochi on a mat with his box
- golgappa cart, vegetable rehri, bangle seller, kabadi cart stacked with scrap
- milk collection centre with steel cans
- bookstall, CD shop, mithai shop (named 6 Nov)

## 5. PEOPLE DOING THINGS. This is the "life".
- men on cots in the shade, one with a radio
- CARROM UNDER THE PEEPAL with onlookers (named twice)
- women at the hand pump, buckets queued, kids splashing
- a woman making gobar cakes; another sifting grain in a soop
- kids: hopscotch (stapu court exists), marbles, rolling a tyre, kite, climbing the mango tree
- old man reading a newspaper on a chabutra
- neighbours in a doorway watching one TV
- someone cutting fodder on a chaff cutter outside the cattle yard
- tractor with trolley parked; bullock cart with its yoke on the ground
- RANGOLI at doorways (flat decals, nearly free)

## 6. VILLAGE FURNITURE.
- kites tangled in wires and trees
- transformer on a concrete platform; a cluster of wires on every pole
- a small shrine under a tree; a majar with green cloth
- anganwadi walls painted with cartoons; school wall painted with the alphabet
- panchayat notice board; PDS shop with a queue
- scarecrow in the field; a thresher by the khalihan
- smoke from the brick kiln chimney

## 7. THE AFTERNOON MOOD. One feeling for the whole village.
Harsh light, deep shade, stillness, haze. People resting in the shade, not standing in
the sun. The dog asleep. A radio low. Lighting and a few placements: nearly free.

## PLACEMENT RULE
Ride every road and lane end to end. Any stretch over about 8 seconds with nothing worth
looking at gets a scene from this list. Scenes go where people gather: the square, the
chaupal, under trees, at the pond and the well, outside shops, in the gullies.

## 8. THINGS THAT HAPPEN TO THE CYCLIST. The best of all, build these early.
- a cow sitting in the middle of the road, you go round it (trivial, do it first)
- a goat herd crossing with a goatherd and a stick
- an unmarked speed breaker, a small jolt on the cycle
- a kid runs alongside the cycle for a few seconds then drops off
- a dog follows you after you ride past it twice
- a tractor overtakes on the highway, honking, "Horn OK Please" on the back
- a kite fight overhead, one cut, drifting into a tree

## 9. THINGS YOU CAN DO. Cheapest first.
ring the temple bell; pump the hand pump; sit on a charpai in the shade; buy a kulhad
chai with steam at the screen edge; sit on the banyan jhoola; switch channels on a radio;
read the panchayat notice board with real notices; feed a cow a roti; pick up a kite.

## 10. SOUND. Free and the biggest lever for "life".
temple bell at intervals; azaan once; a pressure cooker whistle from a house; the thump of
a diesel tubewell engine; chakki hum; cycle bell; crows, a koel, a peacock cry, a buffalo;
a truck horn on the highway.

## 11. EVERY VILLAGE HAS ONE OF THESE.
- a ruined house with a tree growing through the roof (the bhootiya ghar)
- a half-built house with iron rods sticking out of the roof
- a banyan with hanging roots to walk between, a swing on it
- a new pukka house beside a crumbling kachcha one
- an old well with a pulley and a bucket on a rope
- a mazar with a green chadar and a lamp
- a shamiana going up for a shaadi, chairs stacked, a band's drum
- sand pile and sieve, stacked bricks, bamboo scaffolding

## 12. WALL WRITING AND SMALL STORIES. Three or four per street.
"yahan peshab karna mana hai" with a god's picture beside it; political slogans and
symbols half painted over; a "PCO STD ISD" board on a shuttered booth; a shaadi card
pasted on a door; chalk cricket score and children's chalk drawings; "To Let" on a locked
house; a cycle with a child seat at the school gate; yesterday's newspaper on the barber's
chair.

## 13. NOSTALGIC OBJECTS, scattered as discoveries.
gola cart with a hand-cranked ice shaver; a Bajaj scooter under a cover; a Rajdoot; a red
letterbox at the post office; a Doordarshan TV in a wooden cabinet; a jeep, an Ambassador,
a water tanker; a bioscope at the mela ground.
