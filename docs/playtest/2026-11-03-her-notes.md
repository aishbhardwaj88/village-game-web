## 3. The roads themselves are badly made. Hard, structural fault.
Her words: "You really need to take care of the road. Roads are half cut, not clean, and
have very sharp edges. Roads don't have such sharp edges. They are usually curved and
round edges."
Screenshot: a junction seen from above. Every road is a flat brown rectangle laid on the
grass. The faults visible in this one shot:
- **Sharp straight-cut edges.** Each strip ends in a hard angular chevron or a clean
  diagonal slice, like a cut sheet of paper lying on a lawn.
- **Half cut.** Strips stop mid-air and start again, leaving notches and bites out of the
  surface. The road surface is not continuous.
- **No junctions.** Where strips cross, they overlap as separate rectangles instead of
  merging into one piece of road.
- **No edge at all.** The road meets the grass on a hard line, with nothing between.
- Timber kerb rails float beside the strips, not following the road edge.

**Fix. This is not a tweak, the roads need rebuilding as roads:**
1. **Round every edge.** A village road's edge is worn, irregular and soft, never a
   straight cut. Vary the edge line along its length.
2. **One continuous surface per road.** No notches, no gaps, no overlapping rectangles.
   Where two roads meet, build a real junction that merges into one surface.
3. **Give the edge a transition.** A road does not meet grass on a line: there is scuffed
   earth, loose gravel and thinning grass for a short band either side.
4. **Kerbs follow the road edge**, including round the curve, or they are left out.
5. Judge it from the player camera at standing height, not from above.

**Related to item 1 (road ending in nothing): treat the whole road network as one job.**

## 10. THE HEADLINE NOTE. The village does not read as a community. Orientation was never
## designed, only copied off the map. Fix the orientation of EVERY place.
Her words: "maybe you have tried to follow the map, but you have randomly placed stuff
without actually accessing how things should really be placed inside a village. what will
look natural to passers-by. How our villages usually built. You have to keep all of that
in mind and maybe you will have to change orientation of the places you have built.
Villages are like community. Here you have made everything face back of each other, that
it is not looking like a communal place at all."

**This supersedes item 7, which was one instance of it.**

### What went wrong, honestly
The map move applied `turn_deg` from her plan mechanically, place by place, and nobody
ever asked "what does this building FACE, and is that right?" A turn angle copied from a
drawing is not an orientation decision. So the village is geometrically correct and
socially wrong.

### The rule. A village is built around shared space, not around a grid.
1. **Every building faces the space people use**: the street, the square, the chaupal,
   the pond, the well. Never a neighbour's back, never a blank field.
2. **Fronts face fronts across a street.** Verandah looks at verandah. That facing pair
   is what makes a street feel inhabited.
3. **Backs go together.** Where backs must exist, press them against another back or
   hide them behind a compound wall.
4. **Public buildings open onto the square**: panchayat, temple, chaupal, school gate,
   the shops. Their doors should all be visible standing in the square.
5. **Homes cluster and face inward** onto a shared courtyard or a common lane, the way
   families who know each other actually build.
6. **The pond, the well and the peepal are social.** Things look at them, not away.

### How to do it, since this is a big job
- Go street by street, not building by building.
- For each street: stand the camera at walking height at each end and in the middle.
  Every front you can see should be a front. Note every back you can see.
- Fix by TURNING, not moving. Turns survive any later map shrink; positions may not.
- Report each street as done with one shot from the player camera.
- **Start with the square and the main street**, because that is where she will look
  first and where "community" is either there or not.

Panchayat and the dark ghar (TV house) currently face each other's backs. Turn them.
