# Spatial common sense. Read before placing ANYTHING. Her words, 7 Nov:
# "You need to use your brain when you're putting things."

What went wrong: the halwai was placed against the zero milestone at the chauraha, with a
well beside it. A chauraha is a crossroads that vehicles TURN AROUND. Nothing may stand in
the turning circle. She called it stupid. She is right. These rules stop it recurring.

## 1. The chauraha (village square / zero milestone)
- **Clear ground for at least 12 m in every direction** from the milestone. A bus, a
  tractor with trolley and a bullock cart must all be able to circle it.
- The milestone and the flag stand ALONE in the middle. Nothing else inside the circle.
- Shops, the chaupal, the peepal and the tea stall sit BACK on the four corner plots,
  fronts facing the circle. The square is ringed by fronts, never filled.
- Vehicle routes through the square go AROUND the milestone, never through it.
- **Fix now: move the halwai and the well out of the circle onto a corner plot, facing in.**

## 2. Every place sits where its USERS arrive
- Bus adda, petrol pump, dharam kanta, puncture shop: ON the highway, fronting it.
- Mandi: fronting the road trucks come in on, with turning room.
- Temple, panchayat, chaupal, school: fronting the village, on the square or main street.
- Gaushala, khalihan, haystacks, tubewell, kiln: at the edge, on the field side.
- Pond and johad: at the low edge, with the dhobi ghat on the road side.
- If you cannot say who arrives at a place and from where, do not place it yet.

## 3. Things need room to be used
- A shop needs a strip in front for customers to stand and a cart to park.
- A hand pump needs a wet apron and a drain; a queue forms on the road side.
- A chaupal needs open ground in front for people to sit; nothing built against it.
- A school gate needs a place for cycles and parents.
- A cattle yard needs a way for the buffalo to walk in from the road.
- Anything vehicles reach (petrol, mandi, kanta, kiln) needs a turning circle.

## 4. Things cluster by kind, and the kinds do not mix at random
- Dirty and noisy at the edge: kiln, kanta, gaushala, dung yard, tubewell engine.
- Trade on the main street and the square.
- Homes on the lanes behind, in rows with gullies, fronts facing fronts.
- Water (pond, well, pumps) where the women's walk is short, never beside the kiln.
- Religious buildings on their own small open ground, not wedged between shops.

## 5. Two things never touch unless they belong together
- A shop never touches a shrine, a milestone, a well or a hand pump.
- A house never backs onto the square.
- A bus adda never sits on a village lane.
- A haystack never stands in the bazaar.
- Before placing anything, name its two nearest neighbours and ask: would these three
  stand like this in a real village? If the answer is no, do not place it.

## 6. The test
Stand at the thing you placed and look around. Can you say out loud why each neighbour is
where it is? If you cannot, you placed it by coordinate, not by sense. Move it.
